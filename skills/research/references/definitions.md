## Awareness levels (Eugene Schwartz)
- **Unaware** – no felt problem; lead with story or identity, never the product
- **Problem-aware** – feels the problem, doesn't know solutions exist; lead with the problem in their words
- **Solution-aware** – knows the category, not the product; lead with the mechanism / why this works where others fail
- **Product-aware** – knows the product, not convinced; lead with proof, offer, objection handling
- **Most aware** – ready; lead with the deal

## Market sophistication stages (Schwartz)
1. First to market: state the claim plainly
2. Competitors arrived: enlarge the claim
3. Claims no longer believed: introduce a mechanism
4. Mechanisms compete: enlarge/specify the mechanism
5. Everything is disbelieved: identify with the prospect, lead with experience and identity

## Default desire trap – 5 checks
Answer yes/no. Any yes means the chosen desire is probably the category default, not the real lever:
1. Is this the same desire the top 3 competitor ads lead with?
2. Would the product page's own hero section already express it?
3. Does the stated desire appear more often in the data than the real desire you identified?
4. Did you pick it before reading the Scene map and Identity statements?
5. Could you swap in any competitor's product name and the desire statement still works?

1. Personal transformation (first-person before/after story)
2. Patient / user encounter (a specific person, observed)
3. Third-party clinical (studies, trials)
4. Historical / documentary (how the mechanism was discovered or used elsewhere)
5. Social proof chain (volume: reviews, counts, "X people")
6. Authority endorsement (expert, institution)
7. Demonstration (show it working, on camera, now)

## Differentiation levers (Phase 3 take-over plans)
Ways to enter an angle a competitor already runs and make it ours:
- **Unique mechanism** – a different, true explanation of why the problem happens or why past fixes failed. Named or phrased so no competitor's mechanism matches.
- **New avatar** – same angle, a person competitors ignore (from the avatar gap map).
- **New villain** – blame something competitors don't (the old fix, a habit, an industry practice), only if usable per the villain map.
- **New proof** – a proof type competitors don't use (demonstration vs testimonial, lab result vs claim).
- **New scene** – open on a specific moment from the scene map instead of the generic problem statement.
- **Specificity** – numbers, timelines, exact situations from VOC instead of general claims.
- **Format** – the angle in a format nobody uses for it (founder story, text wall, side-by-side test).
- **Sophistication step-up** – if competitors make a plain claim (stage 2), add a mechanism (stage 3); if they have a mechanism, specify or enlarge it (stage 4); if everything is disbelieved, lead with identity and experience (stage 5).

## Course additions (David Briggs) – see course-playbook.md

**Hook frameworks** (with best awareness level): curiosity gap (unaware, problem-aware) · objection flip (solution-aware, skeptical) · mechanism reveal (solution-aware, stage 3+) · results statement (product-aware, sourced numbers only) · self-recognition / call-out (problem-aware, burned before) · contrarian claim (stage 4-5) · story opening (unaware, problem-aware) · direct challenge (strong-assumption audiences)

**Open loop types:** unanswered question · incomplete reveal · unexpected claim · story interrupt · mechanism tease

**Angle types (course list):** fear/loss · aspiration/identity · education/mechanism · social proof/authority · contrarian · comparison · story/transformation · urgency/timing

**Objection strategies:** preempt (near-universal only) · reframe · outprove · ignore (non-fit people)

**Mechanism types:** existing · unspoken (competitors have it, nobody named it) · transubstantiated (ordinary feature, new name or meaning; must still be a real function)

**Story arcs:** against all odds · real-world desperation · discovery

**Social proof hierarchy:** UGC video > UGC images > text testimonials > named buyers, specific stats, review counts, third-party tests, media mentions, visible guarantee
